Why exact size beats "Low / Medium / High"

Most compressors ask you to pick a quality preset and hope the output fits. That is backwards: the platform doesn't care about your quality setting — it cares about megabytes. A 3-minute 4K clip on "Medium" can still be 80 MB, and Discord will still reject it.

VidSizer starts from the number that actually matters. It reads your video's duration, solves for the exact bitrate that fits your target (), then verifies the output and re-encodes once more if it overshoots. If your clip is simply too long for the target, it tells you honestly before you waste time — instead of silently handing you a 400p mess.

FAQ

Is VidSizer really free?

Yes. Because compression happens on your own device, there is no server bill to cover — so there is nothing to charge for. No account, no watermark, no capped "free tier".

Does my video get uploaded to your servers?

No. There are no servers involved at all. The video encoder (FFmpeg) is downloaded once into your browser and runs locally. Your file never leaves your device.

How can the output be an "exact" size?

File size ≈ bitrate × duration. VidSizer measures your video's duration, subtracts the audio track's share and a 6% safety margin, and solves for the video bitrate that lands just under your target. If the budget is tight it steps down the resolution ladder (1080p → 720p → 480p → 360p) instead of starving pixels. After encoding it checks the real file size and re-encodes once more if it overshoots.

What formats can I upload? What do I get back?

MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV and most common formats work as input. Output is always MP4 (H.264 video + AAC audio) with faststart enabled, so it plays everywhere and uploads cleanly.

Will quality suffer?

It depends on the math: a 30-second clip at 20 MB looks great; a 20-minute clip at 20 MB cannot. VidSizer warns you honestly when the target is unrealistic for the clip length, and suggests a bigger target or trimming.

Why is my video scaled to 720p?

For small targets (like 20 MB), keeping 1080p or 4K resolution forces an extremely low bitrate per pixel, which looks worse than a clean 720p encode. Videos taller than 720p are scaled down; smaller videos keep their native resolution.
